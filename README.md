# Automated Essay Scoring of College Application Essays with LLMs

This study explores the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) for automated essay scoring (AES) in the context of college admissions, where evaluation extends beyond grammar and coherence to include personal voice, narrative structure, and emotional resonance. We develop a domain-specific evaluation framework inspired by professional admissions consulting practices, scoring 1,177 Common App essays across five dimensions: Problem/Solution/Takeaway (PST) Ratio, Specificity, Pace, VSPICE (a composite of six character traits), and Creativity. Using GPT-4o and rubric-informed prompts, we extract trait scores that reflect the narrative and stylistic strengths of each essay. These scores are combined with dense semantic representations generated by a sentence-level BERT encoder to form a hybrid feature vector. We evaluate multiple modeling pipelines, ranging from trait-only regressors to hybrid models incorporating both handcrafted features and transformer embeddings. Our final architecture employs a random forest regressor trained on this hybrid vector to predict human-assigned holistic scores. In addition to measuring model performance, we analyze the relationship between individual trait scores and overall ratings. We find that all five traits positively correlate with the holistic score and that essays receiving a low score in any single trait tend to exhibit both a lower median and a lower ceiling in overall scores. Although our results highlight key structural and stylistic factors that influence essay quality, they also underscore the complexity of modeling human judgment in high-stakes and narrative-driven contexts. Rather than replacing human reviewers, we envision this approach as a tool to provide formative feedback and support students through an iterative revision process. By making AES systems more interpretable and aligned with expert evaluation criteria, this work offers a foundation for future research at the intersection of education and natural language processing.
